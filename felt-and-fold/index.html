About the coach

After each hand you played, the coach looks at every decision you made. It compares your action with a baseline strategy. Each decision gets a grade and a number: the big blinds you gave up against that baseline.

Where the baseline comes from

Before the flop, the baseline is a set of opening, 3-bet and defence charts. Short stacks in tournaments use Nash push-or-fold tables.

After the flop, the baseline is usually an estimate. It weighs your equity against the range your opponent likely holds, the pot odds and the board. It is a good guide. It is not a solver.

Heads-up pots can get a real solver answer. Turn and river spots are solved live while you read the review. Some flops come from a library of solved spots. Pots with three or more players always use the estimate.

The labels

Solved
Solved for this exact spot, from the start of the street, with ranges from the hand so far.
Library
Read from a solved flop with the same structure. Suits are mapped onto your board, and sizes are scaled to your pot.
Estimate
The heuristic baseline. Treat the grade as advice. An Estimate grade stops at Mistake unless the pot odds settle it.
Chart
From the preflop chart for this position and stack depth.
Nash
From the Nash push/fold ranges for 15bb and under.

Each decision shows the baseline's mix of plays as a bar. The bar's colours are actions, not grades: check, call, bet or raise (lighter for smaller sizes), all in and fold. The marker shows your play. If the baseline plays an action some of the time, choosing it is fine.

Each grade comes with a reason. Tap an underlined term in it to see what the term means.

The players

Opponents come in five tiers, from Beginner to Solver, and seven styles. Each one adapts a little to how you play. Their names are made up.

Live solving is skipped on phones. Your settings, bankroll and records are saved in this browser only.
